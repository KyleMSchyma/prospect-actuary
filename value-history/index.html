      How good were the values?

      Every Tuesday's values are scored against what the players actually did afterwards. Redraft: points scored over replacement in the rest of that season. Dynasty: points over replacement in the rest of that season plus the next five, discounted 15% a year, the same window the values cover. Dynasty values from 2021 on are only partly scored, because those five seasons are not over yet, so they are shown separately.

      • Rank match (Spearman correlation, 0 to 1): how closely our order matched the order players actually finished in. Higher is better.
      • Simple baseline: the same thing using only this season's points per game, with no credibility weighting, injuries, aging or chance of staying in the league. If we cannot beat it, the model is not earning its keep.
      • Top 24 kept: of our top 24 that week, how many finished in the actual top 24.
      • Predicted / actual: total value we expected divided by total value that happened. 1.00 is right on; above 1 means we were too high.
      • Only players with 3+ games that season are scored, the same rule our model tests use.

      What "only what was known" means

      • Data: each Tuesday uses NFL games played through the weekend before it, that week's injury report and roster status, and the depth chart from the week just played. Draft slots count once the draft has happened.
      • Models: the credibility weights, the draft-capital starting point, the injury and role models and the aging and staying-in-the-league models are refit for each season using only earlier seasons, then held fixed all season. They never see a game from the season being valued.
      • Season length: 16 games before 2021 and 17 after. A 2020 value assumes future seasons have 16 games, because the 17-game season was not announced until March 2021.
      • Same code as today: the history runs today's value scripts with a short list of date edits. Run on this season's data, they reproduce the live board exactly.

      What is not fully clean, and why: the model's design (which inputs to use) was chosen in 2026 with hindsight. NFL stats include official stat corrections made after the games. Team codes are today's (for example LV for the Raiders in 2016). Our stats start in 2006, so values from 2016 to 2018 were fit on fewer seasons than today's model. Injury reports and depth charts start in 2012. Rookie draft picks are not in the history, because we have no mock drafts from those years.

      Data: NFL stats, rosters, injury reports and depth charts from nflverse (via nflreadr), shared under CC BY 4.0. League: 12 teams, superflex, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, no flex, full PPR (the league our weekly archive uses). Method and code: Trade_Values/history.

Why "actuary"

About the Approach

An actuary's job is to put a fair price on an uncertain future: how likely something is, how much it is worth if it happens, and how sure we can be. A draft prospect and a dynasty trade are the same kind of problem. This site borrows the insurance toolkit and applies it to fantasy football.

Four ideas the site is built on

  • Chances, not verdicts. No one knows whether a rookie will hit. A grade here is a probability, and it is judged the way a forecast should be: when we say 3 in 10, do about 3 in 10 hit? The guide shows the answer.
  • Credibility. Insurers blend a customer's own short history with what is typical for people like them, leaning on the history more as it grows. The trade values do the same with a player's season: after about five games this season counts as much as everything before it, and his past seasons and draft slot fill the gap until then.
  • Present value. A pension is worth the sum of its future payments, each weighted by the chance it is collected and discounted for time. A dynasty value is the same sum: each future season, weighted by the chance the player is still producing, with later seasons counting less. Tap any player on the Trade Values page to see his value split by season.
  • Value over the alternative. What matters is not how many points a player scores but how many more than the player you could start instead. That is why every value depends on your league's lineup and scoring.

How it is kept honest

  • Simple models win until proven otherwise. Every model is compared with a plain benchmark, usually "just use the draft pick". Fancier methods were tried and dropped when they did not beat it on players the model had never seen.
  • Tested forward in time. The strict tests train only on draft classes whose results were already known, then grade the next class, the way the model has to work in real life.
  • A public record. Grades for the 2024 to 2026 classes were frozen on November 13, 2026 and are scored as seasons come in on the Track record page, misses included.
  • Stated limits. What the models rely on and what they cannot see is written down in Limits and what we rely on.

What this site will not tell you

It will not tell you a player is a sure thing, and it does not watch film. The numbers come from public data: college production, age, recruiting, draft position, NFL stats, depth charts and injury reports. Treat them as a well-tested second opinion beside your own judgment.

The methods follow the Society of Actuaries' predictive analytics coursework. The research code and results are public in the Football repository.

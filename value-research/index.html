Market Check

How do our trade values line up with the market? This page ranks every player by our model and by a market source, all in superflex, and shows where the two disagree, overall and within each position. Pick the source: Dynasty Daddy (dynasty and redraft trade values from its public API), DynastyProcess (open dynasty values built from expert consensus rankings), or the Trade market (dynasty values implied by over a million real trades from the Dynasty Daddy Trade Database, ranked the same way).

Fixed league: 12 teams, superflex, half PPR (0.5), 4-point passing TDs, 2 RB / 3 WR / 1 TE, no flex. This page always uses it, whatever league you saved elsewhere, so every source is measured the same way.

TRADE MARKET: WHERE IT DOESN'T ADD UPThese values come from what managers actually accepted in real dynasty trades (1-for-1, 1-for-2, 2-for-2 and 2-for-3 deals), put on the same curve as our values so the two read the same way. The market doesn't follow an additive scale in every situation:
  • One player for two: the single player usually fetches about 20% more than the two pieces add up to.
  • Two for three: the side with fewer players fetches about 5% more.
  • Bigger packages (1-for-3, 1-for-4, 3-for-4 and up) weren't used. There the best single player fetches 50% or more over the sum, so adding values up isn't reliable.
  • Straight-up swaps between close players: managers trade players as closer in value than this curve spaces them, so small gaps are often an even swap in practice.
  • League settings: traders barely adjust for TE premium, PPR or league size, and price superflex about half as strongly as our model. Pass TD and first-down bonuses aren't in the trade data.
  • Recent news: trades from the last week or so count most, so values react quickly to news and hype.
  • Rarely traded players (under 25 trades) aren't valued on their own and don't appear. Dynasty only. 2027 picks have one generic price per round until the draft order is set (see the picks table).
  • Compared by rank: because of all this, the page uses only the market's order. Each player is compared by overall and positional rank, and 2-for-1 moves give each player our value at his market rank.

            How to read it

            • Our side is the Trade Values page set to a 12-team superflex league with 0.5 PPR, 4-point passing TDs, and 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE starters, no flex. Dynasty projects fantasy points per game over replacement for the rest of 2026 through 2030; redraft covers the rest of 2026 only.
            • Dynasty Daddy values come from its public values API (superflex trade values, dynasty and redraft), saved with the site and refreshed each week rather than pulled on every visit.
            • DynastyProcess values are its superflex value (value_2qb), which turns FantasyPros expert consensus dynasty rankings into trade values.
            • Trade market values are solved so that real trades balance, with recent trades counting most (half-life about a week) and adjustments for league size, superflex, PPR, TE premium and starters, set here to this page's league. Only their order is used: players are compared by rank, like the other sources. Used with permission from Dynasty Daddy's owner; only trade counts and fitted values are published, never individual trades or leagues.
            • Dynasty Daddy and DynastyProcess don't change with your scoring settings, so small gaps can come from scoring alone.
            • 2027 rookie picks (rounds 1-4) have their own table. The market prices a generic pick per round (DynastyProcess: early, mid and late) until the draft order is set, so each round is ranked among the market's players and compared with our slots in that round, ranked among ours. The player tables leave picks out.
            • A big gap isn't a mistake on either side. It flags a player worth a closer look: our model may see production the market hasn't priced in, or the market may know something (role change, injury news, draft pedigree) the numbers don't capture yet.

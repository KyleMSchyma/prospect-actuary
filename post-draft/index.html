Rankings board

Rank is within position across every class from 2010 to 2026, plus the 2027 class graded from the mock draft above (purple rows; the pick shown is the mock pick).

The grade is a rank within position: an A QB and an A tight end are both near the top of their own position, not worth the same. To compare across positions, use Value: each player's expected dynasty trade value, on the same 100,000-point scale as the trade values page, for your league (default: 12 teams, superflex, PPR, 2 RB / 3 WR / 1 TE, no flex).

How Value is calculated

Value = the chance of each fantasy tier from this post-draft model times what past players in that tier scored over their first four seasons, above replacement, discounted and adjusted for age. The All tab is ordered by Value; each position tab by score. For past classes it's the value at draft time in today's league, not what the player is worth now. It follows the league you set on the Trade Values page (teams, scoring, TE premium, superflex, starters, flex, first-down bonus; saved in this browser); with none saved it uses the default league.

2027 class, graded from a mock draft

Nobody in the 2027 class has been drafted yet, so a mock draft stands in for the draft. Each QB, RB, WR and TE takes his mock pick as his draft capital and his mock team as his landing spot, then goes through the same post-draft model as every drafted player. The pre-draft grade is shown next to it for comparison. These grades will move as the mocks change, and they become real grades after the draft in April.

Source: Renner 2027 Mock Draft (post-trade-deadline), fantasy positions only (30 of its 96 picks, rounds 1-3). The picks and teams come from the mock. The grades are Prospect Actuary's.

Some inputs are estimates. The combine hasn't happened, so every player is treated as an average tester at his position. Age comes from years since high school. The landing spot uses the team's 2026 stats and assumes nobody leaves. When the 2024-2026 classes were scored the same way, the estimate ranked players much like their real post-draft scores did (rank correlation .91 to .98 by position), and the grade is calibrated to that check. "Move" is the number of grade steps from the pre-draft grade (+ means the mock's draft slot and landing spot raised him). Data: pd_post_2027_mock_grades.csv.

Does it work?

Hit rates by grade, the grade scale, grade vs draft slot, and the biggest misses

What the grades show

  • The grade sorts the whole range, top to bottom. A-range players hit far more often than B-range, B more than C, and D/F players almost never reach a tier. The late hits that do get through are rare: Puka Nacua (D), Hunter Renfrow (F), Aaron Jones and Alfred Morris (D).
  • For QBs it beats draft slot alone. QB AUC is 0.88-0.91 against 0.84-0.88 for pick number, and adding the grade to log(pick) improves the QB16 and QB24 fit (likelihood-ratio p = 0.005 and 0.0004).
  • For RB, WR and TE it is about the same as draft slot. AUC is 0.79-0.85 against 0.80-0.86 for pick alone, and the grade adds nothing significant on top of log(pick). Once the pick is known, the college profile and landing spot add little for these positions.
  • Within the late rounds, the grade still sorts players. Among Rounds 4-7 picks, B-range players hit at 15-29% versus 0-13% for C and below (table below).

The grade scale

Score = expected tier points = 3 × P(top tier) + 2 × P(middle tier) + 1 × P(bottom tier), which equals P(top) + P(top two) + P(any tier). The grade is the percentile of that score among drafted players at the same position in 2010-2023. The cut points never move, so a B+ in 2012 means the same as a B+ in 2026.

Tiers follow your cutoffs: QB and RB top 8 / 16 / 24, TE top 6 / 12 / 18, WR top 12 / 24 / 36, judged on the best PPR finish in NFL years 1-3. Historical scores are leave-one-class-out (each class is scored by a model that never saw it); 2024-2026 use the final model trained on 2010-2023.

Method and caveats

Model, coverage, data files

The full explanation, in plain English, is in the guide.

  • Model: the post-draft tier ladder from each position model (QB, RB and TE phase 4; WR phase 6), an ordinal elastic net on the college profile and age plus log(pick) and landing spot (vacated target, touch or attempt share, and incumbent production).
  • Coverage: drafted players need a college stats match in the position model, so a few drafted players are missing (for example Cam Newton 2010).
  • 2024 and 2025 outcomes are best finish so far (two and one NFL seasons); 2026 rookies are mid-season and have no outcome yet.
  • Hit rates per grade rest on 8-67 players per cell; the bootstrap intervals in the chart are wide, especially for QB and TE.
  • Files: pd_post_player_grades.csv (every row on this page, plus the pre-draft grade for reference), pd_post_grade_scale.csv, pd_post_grade_validation.csv, pd_post_grade_vs_pick.csv, pd_post_grade_by_round.csv, pd_post_2027_mock_grades.csv (the 2027 mock-draft section). All of them are in the post-draft/data folder of the Prospect Actuary repository.

Post-Draft Player Grades

Every drafted QB, RB, WR and TE from the 2010-2026 classes, scored by the post-draft model, ranked within position and given a letter grade on one fixed scale. NFL results sit next to every historical player so the grades can be checked. 2027 is not on this board because nobody has been drafted yet; see the pre-draft player grades for that.

What the grades show

  • The grade sorts the whole range, top to bottom. A-range players hit far more often than B-range, B more than C, and D/F players almost never reach a tier. The late hits that do get through are rare: Puka Nacua (D), Hunter Renfrow (F), Aaron Jones and Alfred Morris (D).
  • For QBs it beats draft slot alone. QB AUC is 0.88-0.91 against 0.84-0.88 for pick number, and adding the grade to log(pick) improves the QB16 and QB24 fit (likelihood-ratio p = 0.005 and 0.0004).
  • For RB, WR and TE it is about the same as draft slot. AUC is 0.79-0.85 against 0.80-0.86 for pick alone, and the grade adds nothing significant on top of log(pick). Once the pick is known, the college profile and landing spot add little for these positions.
  • Within the late rounds, the grade still sorts players. Among Rounds 4-7 picks, B-range players hit at 15-29% versus 0-13% for C and below (table below).

The grade scale

Score = expected tier points = 3 × P(top tier) + 2 × P(middle tier) + 1 × P(bottom tier), which equals P(top) + P(top two) + P(any tier). The grade is the percentile of that score among drafted players at the same position in 2010-2023. The cut points never move, so a B+ in 2012 means the same as a B+ in 2026.

Tiers follow your cutoffs: QB, RB and TE top 8 / 16 / 24, WR top 12 / 24 / 36, judged on the best PPR finish in NFL years 1-3. Historical scores are leave-one-class-out (each class is scored by a model that never saw it); 2024-2026 use the final model trained on 2010-2023.

Where the grade misses

D or F who reached the top tier

  • High-grade misses are mostly early picks whose NFL careers stalled (Zach Wilson, Trey Lance, Kevin White, John Ross, Treylon Burks). The model trusts a high pick, so it shares the NFL's mistakes.
  • Low-grade hits are late picks who won a role anyway. There are only a handful, because a late pick and a crowded depth chart rarely lead to a top-tier season.

Method and caveats

  • Model: the post-draft tier ladder from each position model (QB, RB and TE phase 4; WR phase 6), an ordinal elastic net on the college profile and age plus log(pick) and landing spot (vacated target, touch or attempt share, and incumbent production).
  • Coverage: drafted players need a college stats match in the position model, so a few drafted players are missing (for example Cam Newton 2010).
  • 2024 and 2025 outcomes are best finish so far (two and one NFL seasons); 2026 rookies are mid-season and have no outcome yet.
  • Hit rates per grade rest on 8-67 players per cell; the bootstrap intervals in the chart are wide, especially for QB and TE.

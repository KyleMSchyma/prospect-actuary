Two different measurements

  • A prospect grade ranks a player's expected outcome and risk within his position: an A quarterback and an A tight end are both near the top of their own position, not worth the same.
  • A value is what a player or pick is worth as a fantasy asset in your league, on one 100,000-point scale that adds up across positions. It moves with your scoring and lineup; grades do not.

What to take from the grades

  • A "hit" means at least one top-tier fantasy season (top 24 at QB and RB, top 18 at TE, top 36 at WR) in a player's first three NFL years.
  • Pre-draft A-range players hit about 59% of the time (roughly 3 in 5), B about 28%, C and below about 13%. Post-draft A-range players hit 66% (2 in 3), and D/F players almost never (4%, or 1 in 25).
  • Every historical grade comes from a model that never saw that draft class. It did learn from later classes, so the hit rates are a little kinder than a true forecast would have been; the stricter test is in the guide.
  • The draft pick still knows more than college stats alone. Treat the grade as a strong flag, not a guarantee.

Data sources

  • College stats, team stats and recruiting ratings: CollegeFootballData.com, via cfbfastR.
  • NFL draft picks, weekly stats, rosters, injuries and depth charts: nflverse, via nflreadr (CC BY 4.0). Draft and combine data originally from Pro Football Reference.
  • Market values for comparison only: Dynasty Daddy (cached weekly snapshot) and DynastyProcess open data.
  • Updates: values refresh every Tuesday during the season after automatic output checks; a failed check publishes nothing, so the last good values stay up.
  • Every grade and value file is in the site repository as CSV.

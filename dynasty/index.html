Dynasty Value Board

Every active NFL QB, RB, WR and TE, drafted or undrafted, valued the way an actuary prices a pension. Each future season is a "payment" of fantasy points above the startable line, weighted by the chance the player is still startable to collect it. A star with two good years left and a young player with a long runway can land on the same value, and the board shows you which is which.

What the model found

The board

  • Score: dynasty value scaled so the top player is 100. Trade value: a separate scale where sums are fair, so two players whose trade values add to 60 equal one player at 60.
  • Win-now: expected points above the startable line over the next three seasons, no discount. Seasons left: expected startable-alive seasons out of the next ten. Alive '29: chance he is still startable-alive in 2029.
  • Players marked 2025 basis have played fewer than 3 games in 2026, so they are valued from their 2025 season.

How it works

Dynasty value = Σt=1..10  0.87t−1 × P(still startable-alive in season t) × E[points above the startable line in season t]
  • Startable means finishing the season at or above QB24, RB24, WR36 or TE12 in full-PPR points (17-game pace). QB24 makes this a superflex board, which is why QBs sit at the top.
  • Points above the line work like an insurance deductible: only points above that season's QB24 / RB24 / WR36 / TE12 count, and a season below the line is worth zero, not a negative.
  • A career "ends" after two straight seasons below the line. Not playing counts as below, so retirement and long injuries are included. The rookie season gets a free pass.
  • The chance of staying alive comes from survival analysis: a discrete-time logistic hazard model averaged with a survival random forest, fit on every QB/RB/WR/TE season since 2007. Kaplan-Meier and Cox models were fit too; Cox was dropped because its key assumption (effects stay constant over time) fails badly here.
  • Expected points come from a Tweedie GLM (the insurance "pure premium" model, built for many zero seasons plus a long tail) averaged with a random forest.
  • Discounting at 15% a year means a season two years out counts 76% as much as next season.

How long startable players last

Kaplan-Meier "life tables": for every player-season that finished startable (2006-2024), the share still startable-alive t seasons later. No player detail goes into these curves; they are the raw history the models start from.

How age changes value

Partial dependence: take every currently startable player, change only his age, and average the score. Running backs and receivers lose about a quarter of their value with every two years; quarterbacks barely move until their 30s.

Does it work?

A backtest on seasons the model never saw: models fit only on 2007-2017 predicted each player's points above the line over the next three seasons, starting from every 2018-2022 season. The benchmark is the simple rule "he will repeat this season three more times."

  • For players who were startable at the time, the model ranks the next three seasons better than the simple rule at every position and cuts the error by about 40%.
  • Across everyone, the simple rule ranks slightly better, because it puts every current starter above every non-starter. The model sometimes bets on a young non-starter's upside, and those bets lost more often than they won in 2018-2022.
  • For the top tenth of predicted values, actual points came in at 100% of expected. Overall, the model ran about 11% high.

Read it with care

  • 2026 is a partial season. It is pro-rated to 17 games. A player having a bad or injured 2026 is one more bad season from "career over," so his value can swing once the season ends.
  • Superflex by design. In a 1QB league, use the position ranks, or knock QBs down.
  • Players on a 2025 basis are not penalized for 2026 yet, so their values lean optimistic.
  • Quarterbacks over 40 are extrapolations; very few players that old are in the history.
  • The model sees production, age, games played, usage and draft round. It does not see contracts, coaching changes, injuries in detail or depth charts.
